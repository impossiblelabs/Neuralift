How it works

Strength isn't only muscle. It's also your thinking.

Every voluntary contraction begins as a signal. How much force you produce depends on the muscle you have, and on how completely your nervous system can recruit and drive it. Neuralift trains the second part.

01 · MOTOR CORTEX

Plan the contraction

Eyes closed, you imagine pulling as hard as you can. Your brain plans the effort as if it were real.

02 · SPINAL CORD

Send the signal

The signal travels from brain to muscle. Practising it strengthens the pathway.

03 · MUSCLE

Recruit the muscle

More of the muscle you already have switches on when you need it. Same muscle, stronger signal.

The research

Scientists got people stronger using imagined contractions alone.

In one peer-reviewed 12-week study, healthy participants practised maximal contractions entirely in their imagination, five days a week. Their measured strength went up.

Ranganathan et al., 2004 · study dose

The 35% result came from little-finger strength in one 12-week study. Elbow-flexion strength increased 13.5% in the same study. Results vary, and physical resistance training produced larger gains.

Alongside the strength changes, the researchers reported measurements consistent with increased cortical drive to the trained muscles. Nothing about the muscle itself had been loaded. What changed was the signal.

The wider literature points the same way, with the same honest limits.

  • Strength improvements after imagined contractions have been reported in several independent studies.
  • Motor imagery has been shown to reduce strength loss during limb immobilisation.
  • Imagery training has been associated with increased motor-cortex activity.
  • Meta-analytic evidence finds motor imagery improves maximal voluntary strength compared with no training.
  • Across the literature, physical resistance training produces larger and more reliable gains.

Ranganathan VK, Siemionow V, Liu JZ, Sahgal V, Yue GH. From mental power to muscle power: gaining strength by using the mind. Neuropsychologia. 2004;42(7):944–956.

One session

What twenty minutes looks like

Every session follows the same shape. Consistency is the point: the study's participants did the same thing, day after day, for twelve weeks.

  • 00:00Settle and focusSit or lie down, eyes closed. Bring attention to the target muscle.
  • 02:00Imagined maximal contractionsSets of 5-second maximal efforts, 5 seconds rest, guided rep by rep.
  • 16:00Imagery quality checkRate how vivid and effortful the contractions felt. This is tracked across the program.
  • 18:00Cool-down and logTwo minutes down, then perceived exertion and adherence are recorded.
  • 20:00DoneSame time tomorrow.
